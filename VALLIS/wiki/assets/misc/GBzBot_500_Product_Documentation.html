2. Strategy logic (how it decides)

Layman version: GBz_Bot waits for the market to become “tradable,” then it only trades when multiple independent signals agree — and it sizes the trade based on how strong that agreement is.

Edge principle: You don’t win by being right all the time — you win by making sure your winners are larger than your losers while avoiding “bad market states.”
Step A — Market state filter (trade/no‑trade)
The bot first asks: is this a clean environment? If volatility is unstable, spreads widen, or the regime is chaotic → it stays out.
Step B — Multi‑factor confirmation stack
A trade requires agreement across a stack (example): trend alignment, momentum confirmation, volatility condition, and a “no trap” rule (avoid obvious fakeouts).
Step C — Confidence score & position sizing
Each confirmed setup produces a confidence score (0–100). Higher confidence → larger size (within caps). Low confidence → smaller size or no trade.
Step D — ATR-based TP/SL + trailing logic
Take profit and stop loss are set from ATR so risk matches market movement. Optional trailing locks profits when the move continues.

Lowest usable: You can technically start as low as $50–$100 in paper/sim mode to prove logic, but live execution realism suffers (fees, min order sizes, and noise). For an operator aiming to actually compound, the practical floor is usually $250–$500 for spot/low leverage systems.

Recommended starting bands:

  • $1,000–$5,000: realistic compounding range (more stable risk % with less fee drag).
  • $10k+: scaling requires pair diversification + execution upgrades (opportunity caps appear).

Over 6–24 months, the growth lever is not “more trades,” it’s: (a) avoiding drawdowns, (b) increasing confidence calibration, and (c) diversifying pairs while keeping risk constant.
